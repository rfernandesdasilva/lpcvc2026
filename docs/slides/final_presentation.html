Better photo search in 17.7 ms on a phone

Image–text retrieval on the Samsung Galaxy S22: model selection, fine-tuning, and what quantization and NPU-aware design actually buy
Rafael Fernandes da Silva · Erick Castillo · Carl Augustin
Project presentation · 19 November 2026
01 · Problem

The task: rank captions for each photo, on the phone, in under 35 ms

Constraint

Image + text encoder latency < 35 ms on a Galaxy S22, compiled and profiled through Qualcomm AI Hub (QNN runtime). Valid entries are ranked by accuracy.

Why the baseline is inefficient

  • CLIP ViT-B/16: 150M parameters; the image tower is 12.7 of its 15.8 ms
  • Trained on web alt-text, not object captions: 63.2 on our object-caption set
  • The official sample skips CLIP's input normalization (worth +1.5 points)

Why it matters: on-device search keeps personal photos private and works offline, but every millisecond on the NPU costs battery.

02 · Prior work

Dual encoders make retrieval cheap; MobileCLIP2 makes the encoders small

CLIP (Radford et al., 2021)

Two separate encoders trained so matching image and caption vectors point the same way (contrastive loss over 400M web pairs).

Retrieval is one dot product per pair, so all the cost sits inside the two encoders, mostly the image tower.

MobileCLIP / MobileCLIP2 (Vasu et al., 2024; Faghri et al., 2025)

"Reinforced" training: distill an ensemble of large CLIP teachers and synthetic captions into small models.

Hybrid conv + transformer image towers. S0's convolution branches are merged into single convs at export: 11.4M image parameters vs CLIP's 86M.

Affinity distillation (TinyCLIP, Wu et al., 2023)

The student copies the teacher's image-to-caption similarity distribution within each batch, alongside the usual contrastive loss.

This is the distillation loss we tested (slide 9).

What this tells us to try: start from a compact model trained this way, adapt it to the competition's caption style, and only then compress further. LPCVC 2025 winners (Ye et al., 2026) also started from pretrained compact CLIP-style models.

03 · Approach: fine-tuning

Fine-tuning on object captions was the one large lever

Recipe (fixed before the first run)

  • 15,905 RefCOCO / RefCOCO+ / RefCOCOg train images; no validation image
  • Multi-positive contrastive loss, 256 images × 2 captions per step
  • 5 epochs (310 steps), AdamW lr 1e-5, frozen temperature, flips only
  • ~22 min on one RTX 4080 SUPER; last epoch reported, no early stopping

Why it works

Pretraining matched photos to whole-scene web text. The competition asks for one object among similar ones ("the purple car"). Fine-tuning teaches attributes and positions; most of the gain arrives in epoch 1.

04 · Results: honest evaluation

Most of the validation gain is RefCOCO caption style, so we claim +4.0 on real LPCVC images

Training on COCO Captions gives about the same gain on every set. Training on RefCOCO-family captions gives a 10× larger gain only on the sets written by the same annotators.

LPCVC images are also easier: salient, distinct objects. A random 56-image subset of our grouped set scores 73.9 for the baseline vs 87.4 on the sample.

What we claim: fine-tuned B beats the official baseline by +4.0 [+1.6, +6.9] on the real LPCVC sample (91.4 vs 87.4, paired, 56 images).

Our sets still rank models trained on the same data correctly (Spearman 0.89–0.99 with the sample), so we keep using them for that.

RefCOCOg-LPCVC, pretrained models, quantized encoder paired with the float one. 256 calibration images / 512 captions.

  • 8-bit activations collapse everything: the residual stream spans about ±500
  • 16-bit activations are lossless, except B's text tower (next slide)
  • On the phone, only B image w8a8 ran; the rest failed to compile or to load

What INT8 does buy: peak memory 166 → 101 MiB (−39%) for B image. On this phone quantization is a memory lever, not a speed lever.

07 · Lessons learned

What we would tell the next team

Measure on the deployed runtime

TFLite put the baseline at 22.2 ms, QNN at 15.8 ms. The NPU's FP16 path is why INT8 bought only 5%.

56 images cannot choose a model

±6 points of noise. Our 1,500- and 1,200-image sets with paired intervals made the decisions.

Your validation set can share your training data's style

+22 on our sets, +2.2 on LPCVC images. Claim only what the real distribution shows.

Distill only from a teacher that is better at the task

A zero-shot teacher 2.6 points ahead cost us 5.6 points.

A quantization collapse can be one bug

A single −∞ constant explained B's text tower; a finite mask fixed it with no loss.

Profile per operator before compressing

One activation was 36% of the work; changing it saved more than quantization.
